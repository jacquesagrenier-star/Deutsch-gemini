A real photograph, taken with a good phone camera and looking exactly like a phone photograph, of the small lobby of a family hotel in Hamburg on a Friday evening, taken as a guest coming in through the entrance a few metres away would take it, in a tall vertical picture, everything in sharp focus. Natural colours, warm light from the lamps and the last blue daylight from a window at the back. The textures of real polished wood, brass, leather, carpet, paper, glass, fabric and skin, with the true depth and the slight lens perspective of a photograph.

The first reference picture shows the young man from the front, the second in three-quarter view: his face, his short hair, his build and his height stay exactly as in them. Use them for the person only; his clothes here are a grey wool coat open over a dark-blue sweater, dark jeans and brown leather shoes.

Every object stands on its own, with a little space around it, so that the eye can tell at once where each thing is. Nothing in the picture carries any writing, numbers or print: the key has a plain wooden tag with no number, the leaflets on the desk are plain folded sheets of coloured paper with no print, the computer screen is dark, the wall clock has a plain face and no numbers, the newspaper shows only grey illegible lines, the passport is a plain dark-red booklet, the doors of the lift are plain brushed metal, and nothing carries a sign, a logo or a brand.

THE RECEPTION DESK stands on the left of the picture, a dark polished wooden desk seen from slightly to the side. On it, from the left of the picture to the right:
- a dark computer screen turned towards the receptionist;
- a black telephone;
- a small brass bell;
- a few plain folded leaflets in a wooden holder;
- a small green plant in a pot;
- a plain registration sheet with a pen lying on it.
Behind the desk, on the wall, a wooden board with a row of brass hooks and a few keys with plain wooden tags hanging on them, and a round wall clock with a plain white face and no numbers.

THE RECEPTIONIST, a man of about forty-five with short grey hair and a neat beard, in a dark suit with a dark-red tie, stands behind the desk and holds out a key with a plain wooden tag across the desk, smiling.

THE YOUNG MAN of the reference pictures stands in front of the desk, seen whole from his head to his shoes, which stand on the dark-red carpet with their shadow beside them. He is caught in the very moment of taking the key: his fingers close on the wooden tag, his other hand holds his dark-red passport, and he smiles back at the receptionist. A grey suitcase stands upright on its wheels beside him, with a black backpack leaning against it.
