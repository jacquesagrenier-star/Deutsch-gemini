A real photograph of the entrance and changing room of an old neighbourhood public bath in Tokyo on a May evening, in a tall vertical picture, caught as it happens, as a regular customer would take it with a good phone camera. Taken with a 24 mm lens at standing eye height from the door of the women's changing room, looking across the room towards the frosted glass doors of the bath, everything in sharp focus. Warm light from old ceiling lamps; the textures of real polished wood, woven rattan, rush matting, frosted glass, steam, terry cloth, glass bottles, cotton, corduroy and skin.

The first reference picture shows the young woman from the front, the second shows her from her side, the third shows the bath-house lady, Mrs Kimura, a Japanese woman of seventy-four: the young woman's face, curly dark-brown hair, build and clothes, the mustard-yellow corduroy jacket, the white t-shirt and the black jeans, and Mrs Kimura's face, short grey permed hair and build, stay exactly as in them.

Every surface in the picture is plain: the walls are bare dark wood and plain cream plaster, the doors of the lockers are plain wood with small wooden keys, the towels are plain white, the milk bottles carry plain blue caps, the clock has a plain white face with two black hands, and the frosted glass is plain milky white. Each object stands clearly on its own, with a band of bare wood, bare matting or bare wall around it, so that the eye can tell at once where everything is.

The picture is built in three bands, from the bottom edge to the top edge.

THE FOREGROUND, along the bottom of the picture:
- the floor is pale rush matting with dark cloth borders; on it, a low wooden bench along the left;
- on the bench sits an old woman in a pale-blue cotton robe tied at the waist, her short white hair still damp, drinking milk from a small glass bottle with a blue cap, a white towel around her neck;
- on the right, a stack of round rattan baskets on the floor, the top one holding a folded white towel and a small white bar of soap;
- in the corner, an old metal floor scale with a round dial.

THE MIDDLE OF THE ROOM:
- on the left wall, a tall wall of small wooden lockers in neat rows, each door with a small wooden key in its lock;
- on the right, the high wooden counter of the bath-house, raised like a little booth; behind it sits Mrs Kimura of the third reference picture, in a dark-indigo cardigan, a white towel over her shoulder, leaning her forearms on the counter, smiling and explaining something with one raised forefinger;
- on the counter in front of her, a small tray with a few coins, and a glass-fronted refrigerator beside the counter with rows of small milk bottles with blue caps;
- in front of the counter, the young woman of the reference pictures stands seen whole from her head to her feet, in her white socks, in three-quarter view, her body turned towards Mrs Kimura on the right of the picture; she holds a folded white towel against her chest with both hands and listens with wide eyes and a nervous smile; her white sneakers stand side by side on the matting by the door behind her.

THE BACK OF THE ROOM, across the upper third of the picture:
- across the back wall, two tall sliding doors of frosted glass in wooden frames, the steam of the bath showing as a soft glow behind them;
- beside them, a wide mirror on the wall above a low shelf with a hair dryer and a comb;
- an old electric fan with a round grille stands on a high wooden shelf in the corner;
- a round white clock with a black rim and two black hands hangs above the glass doors;
- the ceiling is dark wood with two old round lamps, lit.

The bath-house is old, clean and warm: between the objects and the people, the same bare wood, the same bare matting and the same plain cream walls.
