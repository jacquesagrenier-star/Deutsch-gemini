The first reference picture is a drawing of a town square in Berlin. Turn it into a real photograph: a photorealistic photograph taken with a 35 mm lens from a first-floor window, on a sunny early afternoon in late spring, with natural colours, warm daylight, soft shadows and the textures of real stone, brick, water, sand, leaves, fabric and skin.

Keep everything exactly where it is in the drawing, with the same sizes and the same layout: the red-brick church with its green spire on the left, the pale sandstone town hall with its staircase and tower, the blue bookshop, the ice-cream parlour with its pink-and-white striped awning, the bronze statue on its stone plinth, the street musician with his guitar, the man with the brown dog, the round fountain with its jet of water, the mother with the dark-blue pram, the playground with the red slide, the swings and the sandpit, the round bed of red and yellow tulips, the chestnut tree, the bench with the old man feeding the pigeons, and the green litter bin.

The young man sitting on the rim of the fountain and waving is the young man of the second and third reference pictures: his face, hair, build and height are exactly as in them. The young woman walking towards him from the left is the young woman of the fourth reference picture: her face, her dark blonde hair tied back in a ponytail, her build and her height are exactly as in it. They keep their clothes, their places and their gestures from the drawing.

Every surface stays plain, as in the drawing: the shop windows show only their goods, the awnings are plain cloth, the plinth and the litter bin are bare.
