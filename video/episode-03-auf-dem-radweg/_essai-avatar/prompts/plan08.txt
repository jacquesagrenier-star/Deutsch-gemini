A static medium shot holds on him, seen from the waist up. The camera is locked on a tripod and stays there for the whole shot: the same lens, the same height, the same distance. Keep his position and his distance from the camera exactly as in the image. His head stays turned three quarters away from the lens, at exactly the angle it has in the image, and it holds that angle from the first frame to the last.

Something behind him has just startled him, and what it is remains a complete mystery to him -- his face says so plainly, eyebrows up, eyes wide and searching. It is pure surprise, open and good-humoured: he is startled the way a cheerful man is startled, already half ready to be amused by whatever it turns out to be.

He turns and asks, to someone just off screen to his RIGHT. Let his face follow the voice.

He turns his body and head to his right in one movement, quickly, to look behind him, and he stays turned that way. This turn is the whole action of the shot.

Finally he stops speaking and holds the look, eyebrows up, the beginning of a smile at his mouth. He blinks, he breathes, and he waits to be told.

The pavement runs on past him to the right, with parked bicycles against a plain building front and a pedestrian light further along. His eyes rest on a fixed point just beyond the right edge of the frame, the way one looks at someone one is speaking to.

The framing holds to the very end: in the last frame he is the same size and in the same place in the picture as in the first.
