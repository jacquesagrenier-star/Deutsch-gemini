A static close shot holds on him, head and shoulders. Locked-off camera: no zoom, no push-in, no camera movement of any kind. Keep his position and his distance from the camera exactly as in the image. His head stays turned three quarters away from the lens, at exactly the angle it has in the image, and it holds that angle from the first frame to the last.

He has just understood that his small crime at the traffic light had a witness, and it delights him. He is caught, he knows it, and he finds it funny.

He asks, half laughing already, to someone just off screen to his RIGHT. Let his face follow the voice.

His eyebrows go up and a real laugh breaks through in the middle of the question, warm and at his own expense.

Finally he stops speaking and the smile stays, open and unembarrassed. He blinks, he breathes, and he is enjoying this.

The pavement runs on past him to the right, with parked bicycles against a plain building front and a pedestrian light further along. His eyes rest on a fixed point just beyond the right edge of the frame, the way one looks at someone one is speaking to.

The framing itself never changes: he stays exactly the same size and in exactly the same place in the frame from the first frame to the last.
