A static close shot holds on him, head and shoulders. The camera is locked on a tripod and stays there for the whole shot: the same lens, the same height, the same distance. Keep his position and his distance from the camera exactly as in the image. His head stays turned three quarters away from the lens, at exactly the angle it has in the image, and it holds that angle from the first frame to the last.

He has just understood that he was seen, and it delights him. He is caught, he knows it, and he finds it funny.

He asks, half laughing already, turning his face toward the right edge of the frame. Let his face follow the voice.

His eyebrows go up and a real laugh breaks through in the middle of the question, warm and at his own expense.

Finally he stops speaking and the smile stays, open and unembarrassed. He blinks, he breathes, and he is enjoying this.

The pavement runs on past him to the right, clear and open all the way, with parked bicycles against a plain building front and a pedestrian light further along. His eyes rest on a fixed point just beyond the right edge of the frame and stay there for the whole shot.

The framing holds to the very end: in the last frame he is the same size and in the same place in the picture as in the first.
