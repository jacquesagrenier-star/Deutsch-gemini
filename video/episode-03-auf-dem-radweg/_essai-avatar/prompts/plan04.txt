A static medium shot holds on her, seen from the waist up. Locked-off camera: no zoom, no push-in, no camera movement of any kind. Keep her position and her distance from the camera exactly as in the image. Her head stays turned three quarters away from the lens, at exactly the angle it has in the image, and it holds that angle from the first frame to the last.

She is indignant and perfectly polite at the same time, and both are plain on her face. She is correcting a stranger because someone has to.

She calls out a rule and then a reason, to someone just off screen to her LEFT. Let her face follow the voice.

One index finger comes up and points firmly toward the traffic light, and it stays up while she speaks.

Finally she stops speaking with the finger still raised, still aimed at the light, and keeps looking at him. She blinks, she breathes, and she waits to see whether he understood.

The pavement runs on past her to the left, with a pedestrian light and parked bicycles behind. Her eyes rest on a fixed point just beyond the left edge of the frame, the way one looks at someone one is speaking to.

The framing itself never changes: she stays exactly the same size and in exactly the same place in the frame from the first frame to the last.
