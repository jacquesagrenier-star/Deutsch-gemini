A static close shot holds on him, head and shoulders. The camera is locked on a tripod and stays there for the whole shot: the same lens, the same height, the same distance. Keep his position and his distance from the camera exactly as in the image. His head stays turned three quarters away from the lens, at exactly the angle it has in the image, and it holds that angle from the first frame to the last.

He is warmer again, almost fatherly now. He adds a second rule the other man was not expecting, and he offers it gently, the way one tells someone something useful rather than something owed.

He tells him one more thing, to someone just off screen to his LEFT. Let his face follow the voice.

Nothing else moves: his hands stay where they are and his head stays still. The stillness is the performance here.

Finally he stops speaking and holds the look a moment longer than he needs to. He blinks, he breathes, and he is waiting for the penny to drop.

Behind him the street goes on, soft and out of focus, and it stays empty. His eyes rest on a fixed point just beyond the left edge of the frame and stay there for the whole shot.

The framing holds to the very end: in the last frame he is the same size and in the same place in the picture as in the first.
