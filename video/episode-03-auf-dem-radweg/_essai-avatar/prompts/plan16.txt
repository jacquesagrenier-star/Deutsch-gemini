A static medium shot holds on him, seen from the waist up. Locked-off camera: no zoom, no push-in, no camera movement of any kind. Keep his position and his distance from the camera exactly as in the image. His head stays turned three quarters away from the lens, at exactly the angle it has in the image, and it holds that angle from the first frame to the last.

He is apologising and he is in a very good mood while he does it. Nothing about this morning has upset him.

He apologises and says what he is doing about it, to someone just off screen to his RIGHT. Let his face follow the voice.

He laughs from the chest as he speaks and his head shakes lightly once, at himself.

Finally he stops speaking, the laugh settles into a wide open smile, and he keeps looking at the man. He blinks, he breathes, and he means the apology.

The pavement runs on past him to the right, with parked bicycles against a plain building front and a pedestrian light further along. His eyes rest on a fixed point just beyond the right edge of the frame, the way one looks at someone one is speaking to.

The framing itself never changes: he stays exactly the same size and in exactly the same place in the frame from the first frame to the last.
