He has been called a Berliner and the word makes him laugh: last week it was a pastry.

He repeats the word as a question, then asks a second one through a laugh. He speaks towards a point just beyond the left edge of the picture, level with his own eyes. Let his face follow the voice.

His hand nearer the left edge of the picture stays resting on the grey post of the street lamp, exactly as in the image, for the whole shot. Only his other hand moves: with the laugh it comes up in front of his chest, the thumb and the index finger a few centimetres apart, the size of a pastry, and it stays there holding the air.

Across his chest we see only his plain dark burgundy sweater, from the first frame to the last.

On the left of the picture, between him and the edge of the frame, the orange litter bin on its grey post stays exactly as it is in the image, and past it the pavement runs on, open and quiet, from the first frame to the last.

Finally he stops speaking and keeps laughing, his mouth closed, the whole week in that laugh. He blinks, he breathes, and he holds that expression the whole time he waits.

Behind him the evening street stays quiet, soft and out of focus, the warm light from the shop window steady on the pavement. His eyes rest on a fixed point just beyond the left edge of the frame and stay there for the whole shot.

The framing holds to the very end: in the last frame he is the same size and in the same place in the picture as in the first.
