He has just seen a perfectly good bottle about to go into a litter bin, and he cannot let it happen. Sharp at first, the voice of a neighbour who stops you because somebody has to.

He calls one sharp word, then says one short sentence, firm and a little indignant. He speaks towards a point just beyond the right edge of the picture, level with his own eyes. Let his face follow the voice.

His hand nearer the left edge of the picture stays closed round the neck of his brown bottle, resting low in his lap, exactly as in the image, for the whole shot. Only his other hand moves: on « Halt! » it comes up fast to the height of his shoulder, flat, palm out towards the right edge of the picture, the stop, and it stays there while he speaks.

Across his chest we see only his plain bright yellow jacket, zipped to the collar, from the first frame to the last.

On the right of the picture, between him and the edge of the frame, the lit shop door and the pavement run on, open and quiet, from the first frame to the last.

Finally he stops speaking and keeps a stern, expectant look, his mouth closed, waiting to see what he does. He blinks, he breathes, and he holds that expression the whole time he waits.

Behind him the evening street stays quiet, soft and out of focus, the warm light from the shop window steady on the pavement. His eyes rest on a fixed point just beyond the right edge of the frame and stay there for the whole shot.

The framing holds to the very end: in the last frame he is the same size and in the same place in the picture as in the first.
