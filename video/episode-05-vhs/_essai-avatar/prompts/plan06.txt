A static medium shot holds on him, seen from the hips up. The camera is locked on a tripod and stays there for the whole shot: the same lens, the same height, the same distance. Keep his position and his distance from the camera exactly as in the image.

Deadpan, the funniest thing in the episode said with no expression at all: to him this is the most obvious rule in the world.

He names the ground floor, lists three floors one after the other, and ends with one plain sentence. He speaks towards a point just beyond the left edge of the picture, level with his own eyes. Let his face follow the voice.

His hand nearer the right edge of the picture stays hanging at his side, closed round a ring of keys, exactly as in the image, for the whole shot. Only his other hand moves: it comes up flat, palm down, at the height of his belt, and as he names the three floors it rises in three small even steps, a few centimetres each, and it stays at the third step, palm down.

Across his chest we see only his plain grey cotton work coat, buttoned to the top, from the first frame to the last.

On the left of the picture, between him and the edge of the frame, the corridor runs on towards the open door of the stairwell, open and quiet, from the first frame to the last.

Finally he stops speaking and holds a flat, patient look, his mouth closed, as if nothing could be simpler. He blinks, he breathes, and he holds that expression the whole time he waits.

Behind him the corridor stays quiet, soft and out of focus: the pale green wall, the row of closed wooden doors, the ceiling lights steady. His eyes rest on a fixed point just beyond the left edge of the frame and stay there for the whole shot.

The framing holds to the very end: in the last frame he is the same size and in the same place in the picture as in the first.
