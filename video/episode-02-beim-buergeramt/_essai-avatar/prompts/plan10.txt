A static close shot holds on him, head and shoulders, the counter out of frame. Locked-off camera: no zoom, no push-in, no camera movement of any kind. Keep his position and his distance from the camera exactly as in the image. He never faces the camera.

He is doing the arithmetic out loud, slowly, the way people check an addition
that will not come out. He is not accusing anyone, and he is not wounded -
part of him is already enjoying how absurd this is. The look he gives is
"you hear it too, right?", shared WITH the other man rather than aimed at
him.

He says the two numbers back, one after the other, speaking to someone off
screen to his RIGHT. Let his face follow the voice - the flatness of the
delivery is already in the recording, and the pause between the two halves is
where the joke lives.

First he says the first number while looking slightly down and away, as if
reading it off something. Then his eyes come back up to the other man for the
second half.

Finally he stops speaking and holds the look, mouth closed, eyebrows raised
just enough to make it a question, with the beginning of a smile at one corner
of his mouth - he finds this funny before he finds it unfair, and the smile
must stay that small. He blinks, he breathes, and he waits for the
correction.

⚠ He is the ONLY person in this shot, at every single moment of it. The person he is speaking to stays completely out of frame and is NEVER seen: no head, no hair, no face, no shoulder, no arm, no back of a head, no blurred figure and no part of anyone else enters the frame at any point, at any edge, in focus or out of focus, near or far. Nobody walks in, nobody passes between him and the camera, and nothing in the background comes forward or grows larger.

The framing itself never changes: he stays exactly the same size and in exactly the same place in the frame from the first frame to the last. The camera does not drift, reframe, or follow him, and the background stays where it is.
