A static close shot holds on him, head and shoulders, the counter out of frame. Locked-off camera: no zoom, no push-in, no camera movement of any kind. Keep his position and his distance from the camera exactly as in the image. He never faces the camera.

This is the shot where he stops understanding. His eyebrows go UP and stay
up, his jaw loosens slightly, and his eyes widen a little: the face of a man
being told something he is sure cannot be right. He is open-faced and a little
lost, and he is still perfectly polite.

⚠ His eyebrows must never draw together or down. No frown, no narrowed eyes,
no tightened jaw, no set mouth - those are the face of irritation, and he is
not irritated. He is not arguing with the rule. He simply cannot believe it
applies the way it has just been described to him.

He repeats the number back as a question and then says something that feels
obvious to him, speaking to someone off screen to his RIGHT. Let his face
follow the voice - the pause between the two sentences is already in the
recording, and it is the most important thing in the shot.

First his head moves back about a centimetre, the way people do when a thing
does not fit. Then his eyes search the other man's face for a correction that
is not coming, and only then does the second sentence arrive, quieter than the
first.

Finally he stops speaking and keeps looking, lips just parted, eyebrows still
up. He blinks, he breathes, and he waits to be told he has misunderstood. The
last thing on his face is hope, not anger.

He is alone in the room, and the room is quiet and empty around him. To his right the counter runs on, bare except for the date stamp and the small INFORMATION sign, and behind it a plain pale-green wall with an empty noticeboard; a tall window stands far to his left. Nothing occupies that side of the frame but the counter, the wall and the daylight. His eyes rest on a fixed point just beyond the right edge of the frame, the way one looks at someone one is speaking to, and they stay on it.

The framing itself never changes: he stays exactly the same size and in exactly the same place in the frame from the first frame to the last. The camera does not drift, reframe, or follow him, and the background stays where it is.
