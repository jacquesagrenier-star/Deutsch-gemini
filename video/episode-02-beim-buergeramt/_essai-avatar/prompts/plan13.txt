A static medium shot holds on him, seen from the waist up behind the counter. Locked-off camera: no zoom, no push-in, no camera movement of any kind. Keep his position and his distance from the camera exactly as in the image. He is right of centre and never faces the camera.

Back to practical matters. This part he can solve, and there is a faint
satisfaction in that - no smile, just a man on familiar ground. But he does
pick the sheet deliberately, and square it once against the counter before he
holds it out: the one thing in this whole conversation he can actually do for
this man. It stays a working gesture, never a kind one. The care is the only
place the kindness shows.

He hands over a form and tells the person what to do with it, speaking to
someone off screen to his LEFT. Let his face follow the voice.

First he squares the sheet once against the counter, then holds it out across
the counter toward the person, arm extended but relaxed, the paper flat
between finger and thumb. Then he says what to do with it.

⚠ THE ARM NEVER COMES BACK. From the moment the sheet is held out it stays
out - the same extension, the same height, the paper fully visible in frame -
all the way to the LAST FRAME of the clip. He does not lower it, does not draw
it back toward himself, does not let it drift down to the counter, and the
sheet never leaves the frame or disappears from view. It simply stays offered,
held at that height, for as long as the clip lasts.

Finally he stops speaking and waits with the sheet still held out, mouth
closed, eyes on the person. He blinks, he breathes, and he waits to see
whether there will be another question.

He is alone in the room, and the room is quiet and empty around him. To his left the counter runs on, bare except for the small INFORMATION sign and the date stamp, and behind it a plain pale-green wall with a closed noticeboard. Nothing occupies that side of the frame but the counter, the wall and the daylight. His eyes rest on a fixed point just beyond the left edge of the frame, the way one looks at someone one is speaking to, and they stay on it.

The framing itself never changes: he stays exactly the same size and in exactly the same place in the frame from the first frame to the last. The camera does not drift, reframe, or follow him, and the background stays where it is.
