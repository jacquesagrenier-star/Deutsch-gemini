A static close shot holds on him, head and shoulders, the counter out of frame. Locked-off camera: no zoom, no push-in, no camera movement of any kind. Keep his position and his distance from the camera exactly as in the image. He never faces the camera.

The long word has defeated him, and he knows it is a good word - he simply
cannot hold it. No shame in it: he speaks German well and this one is beyond
reach. The shot is the funniest in the episode and HE IS IN ON IT - he is the
first person to find it funny, and he is laughing at himself, never
embarrassed. This is the moment the audience decides they like him.

He starts to repeat the word, gives up in the middle, and asks again, speaking
to someone off screen to his RIGHT. Let his face follow the voice - the break
in the middle is already in the recording and it is the whole shot.

First his mouth starts the word with some confidence. Then it stops, and for a
moment nothing happens on his face at all. Only after that does the rest of
the question arrive, smaller.

Finally he stops speaking and a helpless half-smile arrives, warm and openly
amused - it must not be there at the start, and it must not be wide, but it
must be genuine: he is delighted by the size of the word that just beat him.
He blinks, he breathes, he waits.

⚠ He is the ONLY person in this shot, at every single moment of it. The person he is speaking to stays completely out of frame and is NEVER seen: no head, no hair, no face, no shoulder, no arm, no back of a head, no blurred figure and no part of anyone else enters the frame at any point, at any edge, in focus or out of focus, near or far. Nobody walks in, nobody passes between him and the camera, and nothing in the background comes forward or grows larger.

The framing itself never changes: he stays exactly the same size and in exactly the same place in the frame from the first frame to the last. The camera does not drift, reframe, or follow him, and the background stays where it is.
