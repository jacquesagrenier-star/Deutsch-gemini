She knows exactly what « ahorita » means, because she is Mexican too: knowing, teasing, fond.

She repeats one word as a question, then says she knows better; one eyebrow rises as she speaks. She speaks towards a point just beyond the left edge of the picture, level with her own eyes. Let her face follow the voice.

Her hand stays on the black handle of her shopping trolley, exactly as in the image, for the whole shot. Only her other hand moves, the one nearer the right edge of the picture, which starts low at her side by the hem of her coat: as she repeats the word it rises to the height of her chest, palm down and fingers spread, and tilts gently from side to side, the « more or less » of the hand, then stays there.

Across her chest we see only her navy coat and her plain white blouse, from the first frame to the last.

On the left of the picture, between her and the edge of the frame, the top of her trolley and the sunny street run on, open and quiet, from the first frame to the last.

Finally she stops speaking and keeps a knowing half-smile, her mouth closed, her eyes steady. She blinks, she breathes, and she holds that expression the whole time she waits.

Behind her the sunny street stays quiet, soft and out of focus. Her eyes rest on a fixed point just beyond the left edge of the frame and stay there for the whole shot.

The framing holds to the very end: in the last frame she is the same size and in the same place in the picture as in the first.
