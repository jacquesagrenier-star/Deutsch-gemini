A static medium shot holds on him, seen from the hips up. The camera is locked on a tripod and stays there for the whole shot: the same lens, the same height, the same distance. Keep his position and his distance from the camera exactly as in the image.

Now the waiter approves: the newcomer has learned. Then comes the question of the trade, three coffees.

He says « that is it » with a nod, then asks the three-choice question briskly. He speaks towards a point just beyond the left edge of the picture, level with his own eyes. Let his face follow the voice.

His hand nearer the left edge of the picture stays flat on the white cloth on the zinc counter, exactly as in the image, for the whole shot. Only his other hand moves: as he names the three coffees it rises in front of his chest with three fingers up, the thumb and the little finger folded in, counting, and it stays there holding them up.

Across his chest we see only his plain white shirt, his plain black waistcoat and his three raised fingers in front of it, from the first frame to the last.

On the left of the picture, between him and the edge of the frame, the zinc counter and the chrome coffee machine run on, bright and still, from the first frame to the last.

Finally he stops speaking and waits for the answer with one eyebrow up, his mouth closed. He blinks, he breathes, and he holds that expression the whole time he waits.

Behind him the white tiled wall of the bar and its shelves of white cups stay soft and out of focus, still, in the warm morning light. His eyes rest on a fixed point just beyond the left edge of the frame and stay there for the whole shot.

The framing holds to the very end: in the last frame he is the same size and in the same place in the picture as in the first.
